Company

We measure the
smallest audible
difference.

A decibel is the smallest change in sound a person can reliably notice. That's the whole thesis: the difference between a conversation that closes and one that doesn't is usually small, specific, and completely invisible unless someone is measuring every call.

The story

It started with a spreadsheet
nobody trusted.

Our founders met at a regional insurance carrier, on opposite sides of the same problem. One ran a quality program scoring six calls per agent per month on a form built in Excel. The other was building the speech infrastructure that could, in principle, have scored all of them.

The gap between those two facts was the company. The technology to analyze every conversation had existed for years. What didn't exist was a product that a regulated enterprise could actually deploy — because every vendor in the category required shipping customer recordings to someone else's cloud, and the security review died there every single time.

So we built the opposite. Overtone runs where your data already lives. The system of record stays inside your walls, audio never persists off your network, and the analysis is applied to every call rather than a sample chosen mostly by convenience.

We've been deliberately quiet while we proved that out with early customers in insurance, banking, and healthcare. This page is the first thing we've said publicly.

Milestones

Four years,
heads down.

No launch party, no category invention, no manifesto. Just a long stretch of getting the hard part right before talking about it.

2022

The problem gets named

Two colleagues at a regional carrier conclude that sampling-based quality management is a technology artifact, not a methodology, and start prototyping on nights and weekends.

2023

AlphaDecibel incorporates in Seattle

Founded with a seed round from investors and angels who had run contact center operations at scale, and knew exactly which promises to be skeptical of.

2024

First production deployment

An auto and home insurance carrier goes live analyzing quote calls. Coverage moves from 1.8% of conversations to 100% in a single quarter, and the first surprise is a top performer skipping a required disclosure.

2025

The on-premises architecture ships

After the third enterprise evaluation stalls on data residency, we rebuild the system of record to run entirely inside the customer's network with stateless inference. It becomes the reason most customers choose us.

2026

Overtone, out of stealth

Nine deployments across insurance, banking, healthcare, and telecom. More than forty million conversations analyzed, and the team roughly doubling.

How we work

Four things we argue about
so customers don't have to.

01

Evidence or it didn't happen

Every score the product produces cites the utterance it came from. We hold ourselves to the same standard internally — no roadmap decision gets made on a story about one customer call, and no benchmark goes on this website that we can't reproduce.

02

The customer's boundary is not negotiable

It would be faster and cheaper to build SaaS-only and tell regulated buyers to get comfortable. We decided early that we'd take the harder architecture instead, and we've turned down deals that would have required loosening it.

03

Agents are the users, not the subject

Analytics pointed at people who never see the output becomes surveillance. We design for the agent first — visible scores, visible rubric, visible evidence, and a dispute path that works. If a feature only makes sense when agents can't see it, we don't ship it.

04

Boring where it counts

The interesting part of this product is the analysis. The deployment, the upgrade path, the failure modes, and the audit trail should all be aggressively boring. Nobody has ever thanked a vendor for a surprising database migration.
